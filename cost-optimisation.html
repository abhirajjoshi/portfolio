CASE STUDY · DESIGNCAFE

Making costs visible enough to fix.

Four cost problems at DesignCafe, and what changed once the right people could see them clearly. No single dashboard solved them. Teams acting on better information, month after month, did.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Costs were recorded. They weren't visible.

Carpenter costs, missing and damaged materials, and project budgets were all tracked somewhere. But they sat in different places, at different levels of detail. Nobody could easily see which projects or causes were driving them, so cost reviews ended in general reminders instead of specific fixes.

The operational challenge

Costs were mostly reviewed as totals. Totals hide the pattern. A few causes, teams or project types usually account for most of the loss.

The approach

Capture cost at project level. Review it every month with the teams responsible. Fix the process behind the biggest causes, then check whether the number moved.

02 / FOUR INITIATIVES

One method, four different problems.

01

Carpenter resource management

Project managers needed a way to request carpenters. Operations needed to track assignment, attendance and cost. About three years ago, Smartsheet recommended buying their resource management tool. Instead, my team and I built it inside the Smartsheet setup we already had. That avoided about ₹16 lakh a year in tool cost.

02

Installation cost

Once carpenter assignment, attendance and cost were visible per project, we could see where installation was running over and why. Over time, installation cost came down from about 8% of project value to 4.5%.

03

Missing and damaged materials

Missing and damaged materials were costing about ₹22 lakh a month across roughly 120 projects. I set up a system to track every case, and a monthly root-cause review with the teams involved. Over three years, the cost fell to about ₹10 lakh a month across around 200 projects. Processing time halved, from 18 days to 9.

04

Project COGS: budget vs actual

I built project-level visibility of cost of goods sold, comparing budget with actual. That showed which projects and cost lines were overrunning, and gave us something specific to investigate. Over two and a half years, margins improved by 6 percentage points.

03 / HOW IT WORKED

A simple loop, run every month.

Capture Every case, at project level
Review Monthly, with the teams
Find causes Group by root cause
Fix Change the process
Check Did the number move?
The tracking made the problem visible. The savings came from teams acting on it, every month.

04 / RESULTS

What changed, in numbers.

₹16L / year Tool cost avoided by building resource management in the existing Smartsheet setup.
8% → 4.5% Installation cost as a share of project value.
+6 pp Project margin, over two and a half years, helped by budget vs actual visibility.
₹22L → ₹10L Monthly cost of missing and damaged materials, while active projects grew from about 120 to 200.
~₹18K → ₹5K The same cost per project per month. Roughly a 70% drop once volume growth is taken into account.
18 → 9 days Time to process a missing or damaged material case.
These outcomes came from several teams changing how they worked, and other factors played a part too. They shouldn't be read as the impact of the tracking systems alone.

05 / WHERE THINGS STAND NOW

Gains need upkeep.

Some of these numbers have moved back recently. Installation cost has risen again with upstream supply-chain disruption. Processing time for missing and damaged materials is now about 14 days, affected by external factors and post-merger integration.

What hasn't gone back is the visibility. We can see the change as it happens, measure it and trace the causes. That's where the next round of fixes starts.

06 / MY ROLE

Making the right information hard to ignore.

What I did

Worked with functional heads and managers to shape each process. Designed the system architecture and data model. Kept the systems running as needs changed. Built the reports and supported the monthly root-cause reviews.

What the teams did

Acted on what they saw. The savings came from project managers and functional teams changing how they worked. My part was making sure they had clear, trustworthy information to act on.

07 / WHAT I LEARNED

A few things I'd carry forward.

Look per project, not just in total

The monthly loss on missing and damaged materials fell by about half. Per project, it fell by about 70%, because volumes grew at the same time. Totals alone would have undersold the change.

Check what you already own

Before buying a tool, see what your current setup can do. Building on Smartsheet saved money and fitted how teams already worked.

A rhythm beats a one-off fix

The root-cause reviews worked because they happened every month. One review finds problems. A regular one changes habits.
