01 / The context

As the business grew, keeping track of everything became harder.

Project information was created and updated across different stages and teams. Production, installation, quality checks, customer feedback and post-handover requests each had their own tracking needs. We needed a more consistent way to manage this information without making every team work in the same way.

The operational challenge

Teams needed reliable information to plan work, follow up on pending activities and identify issues. As project volumes increased, disconnected trackers and manual reporting became harder to maintain.

The approach

Build a project-level tracking architecture, then develop additional systems around the requirements of individual processes. The aim was to make information easier to capture, maintain and use.

04 / Featured workflow

Making project manager allocation less dependent on manual coordination.

A kickoff meeting brings the designer, project manager, project owner and, where needed, specialist vendors together at the site. The team checks the drawings against actual site conditions and agrees on the execution details before work moves ahead.

Kickoff allocation and coordination

Scheduling these meetings involves more than finding an available project manager. Location, project value, existing workload and the number of kickoffs already handled that month all need to be considered.

1. Request submission

The designer discusses a suitable date with the client and submits project details through an internal tool. This triggers an email to the Execution Team Lead.

2. PM recommendations

The Team Lead enters the project details into a NotebookLM-assisted allocation tool. It ranks suitable PMs based on location, workload, project value and monthly kickoff allocation.

3. Assignment and confirmation

The Team Lead reviews the recommendations and assigns a PM. The designer and PM then coordinate to confirm the date with the client.

4. Kickoff and meeting notes

The team validates the drawings at the site and marks out the proposed modular units. The PM prepares the minutes of meeting using an in-built tool.

Planned enhancement

From PM recommendations to a shared scheduling view

The next step is a Google Apps Script tool that brings allocation and calendar visibility together. The plan is to extract project details from the request email, incorporate PM recommendations and give the Team Lead a calendar view of assignments and availability. This is a planned enhancement, not a deployed feature.

05 / Business impact

Better tracking also changed how operational issues were handled.

The systems were designed to improve visibility and follow-through. Across the wider operations work, this was reflected in delivery adherence, customer feedback and the time taken to resolve service issues.

55% → 92% TAT adherence improved over four years, alongside the expansion of the project tracking ecosystem.
18 → 9 days Resolution turnaround time for missing and damaged material requests, following workflow automation.
₹22L → ₹10L Monthly costs associated with missing and damaged material requests, following process improvements.
-37 → +20 NPS movement between Oct–Dec 2022 and Jan–Mar 2026, alongside the customer feedback programme.
400+ Monthly post-handover maintenance requests handled through the workflow.
₹16L Annual external tool spend eliminated through the internal tracking ecosystem.

These figures reflect outcomes across related operational initiatives during the period. They should not be interpreted as the impact of the project tracker alone.

06 / My contribution

From building the systems myself to leading the team behind them.

My role changed as the architecture and the business grew. I moved from hands-on implementation to setting direction, designing solutions and leading the team responsible for keeping the systems running and improving them.

2018–2022 | Hands-on building

I worked directly with operational teams to understand their problems, rethink processes and build the solutions myself. This included data models, Smartsheet architecture, formulas, automations, reports and dashboards.

2022 onwards | Architecture and leadership

I began working more closely with functional heads and managers on process design and solution planning. I now define the architecture and data models, guide implementation and lead the team responsible for execution, maintenance and development.

The bigger picture

The work was never just about creating more trackers. It was about understanding how teams operate, identifying where information gets lost and building practical systems that help people make better decisions and follow through.
